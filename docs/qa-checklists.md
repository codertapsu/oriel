# Manual QA checklists

## First look at a new build

On the installed app, screen unlocked, after granting Accessibility and Screen & System Audio Recording (quit and reopen after the second). Items 1–15 and the last take about 20 minutes. Items 16–38 cover the features and tools of `docs/reports/03-roadmap-and-tools.md`; Upload (item 28) needs the accounts and servers it names and about 30 minutes, and settings sync (item 38) a second Mac. Items 39–43 cover the fixes of `docs/reports/04-first-use-fixes.md` (about 15 minutes, an administrator's password for item 42), and item 44 Read as Administrator's helper of `docs/reports/05-administrator-helper.md` (about 10 minutes, an administrator's password).

1. **Install.** Open the dmg on a Mac that did not build it: no Gatekeeper warning; the welcome window lists the permissions and **Done** closes it.
2. **Move to Applications.** With no Oriel in Applications, open Oriel straight from the mounted dmg and choose **Move to Applications**: a moment later exactly one Oriel runs, from /Applications (`pgrep -lf Oriel`), with its menu-bar icon.
3. **Menu bar.** The bay-window icon opens a menu with every action and its shortcut (Capture with Timer, Record Window and Pinned among them); Settings opens on General.
4. **Switcher.** Hold ⌥, press ⇥ repeatedly, release: the selected window comes forward. Type to search, Esc cancels, a minimized window comes back when chosen, and a window on another Space switches Spaces. With two displays, set Settings → Window Switcher → Show on to "Display with the active window": the switcher opens on the display of the app in front, wherever the pointer is.
5. **Capture Area (⌃⇧4).** The screen freezes; drag, nudge with the arrows, press Return. The Quick Access Overlay appears; try Copy, Save, Pin (it floats; menu → Pinned lists it and brings it to the front; Close All Pins closes it) and Copy Text on a capture with text.
6. **Live capture.** Turn off Settings → Capture → Freeze the screen while selecting, play a video, Capture Area: the video keeps playing while you drag, there is no loupe, and the shot shows the frame at Return, without the overlay's dimming. Turn the setting back on.
7. **Capture with Timer** (menu): open a menu in another app during the countdown; the area selection then shows it, and so does the shot. The countdown's Cancel button stops it.
8. **Editor.** Annotate from the overlay: add an arrow, text, a blur and a crop; undo and redo; ⌘Y shows the result in Quick Look (Esc closes it); ⌘E exports a PNG; ⌘S saves an `.oriel` file that opens again from Finder.
9. **Window and screen (⌃⇧5, ⌃⇧3); scrolling (⌃⇧6)** on a long Safari page: one tall image without repeated rows.
10. **Recording (⌃⇧R).** Select an area, record 5 s with the control bar's pause in the middle, stop: the finished panel plays it, trims it and exports a GIF. Then **Record Window** (menu): click a window, record a few seconds with a click or two, stop: the movie shows that window's area, with the click rings.
11. **Translate Area (⌃⇧T)** on English text with Japanese as the target: blocks fill in over the text; ⌥ peeks at the original; with no language pack, Oriel offers the download.
12. **Google glossary** (needs a Google Cloud project with a glossary): Settings → Translation → Google → enter the glossary, **Add Service Account Key…** with the key's JSON file; Test Connection reports the glossary's entries, and a translation uses its terms.
13. **Settings.** Every pane opens; change a shortcut in Shortcuts and use it; Advanced → Diagnostics → Copy as JSON.
14. **Clipboard History.** Copy text in Safari: macOS asks whether Oriel may paste from Safari; choose Allow. Oriel opens Paste from Other Apps once; set Oriel to Allow. Then copy text in Safari, an image in Preview, a file in Finder, `#FF8800` and a link. In VS Code, press ⌥V: the panel opens over VS Code with "Paste to Code" and the newest copy selected; each preview shows its kind. Return pastes into the editor; ⌥V again, ⌘2 pastes the second item; in a Messenger chat and in Chrome's search field Return pastes too. Search finds the text inside the copied image. Pin an item (⌘P), drag it within Pinned, delete one (⌘⌫) and Undo; ⌥⌘↓ moves an item. Copy in 1Password: nothing new appears. Capture Area, then Esc on Quick Access: ⌥V lists the screenshot first. Turn on Settings → Clipboard History → Remove items from the history after pasting them: a pasted item leaves the list, a pinned one stays. `oriel debug clipboard --json` shows `access: allowed` and the counts.
15. **CLI.** Advanced → Command-line tool → Install, then `oriel windows list`, `oriel capture area --rect 100,100,400,300 --to clipboard` and `oriel capture area --rect 100,100,400,300 --delay 3 --to clipboard` (a countdown first) in Terminal.
16. **Switcher presets.** Settings → Window Switcher: every shortcut row shows its preset next to its keys; shortcuts made by the earlier build show All windows (⌥⇥) or Active app's windows. Set Shortcut 1 to **Windows grouped by app**: ⌥⇥ shows each app's windows under its name, in thumbnails and in titles; app icons look as before; a quick ⌥⇥ still switches to the previous window. **This display's windows** with two displays: only the windows of the display the switcher opens on. Open the switcher over TextEdit with an unsaved document selected and press ⌥Q: TextEdit asks to save. The line under the windows reads "⌥W Close ⌥M Minimize ⌥F Full Screen ⌥Q Quit App"; turn it off in Appearance.
17. **Capture Text (⌃⇧O).** Drag over a paragraph in Safari: the toast shows its start and the line count, and ⌘V in TextEdit pastes it with its line breaks. Do it again on a two-column PDF page in Preview (the left column comes first), on a form or a table in System Settings (rows with tabs), and on text with a word hyphenated at a line end. Click a window: all its text is read. Hold ⇧ while letting go: the panel opens; edit, ⌘Return copies. ⌥V lists the text in Clipboard History. Over an empty area: "No text found". The first read after launching may show "Reading text…". In Terminal, `oriel capture text --rect 100,100,400,300 --json` prints the text and each line in points.
18. **Instant Capture (⌃⇧2).** Drag over part of a window: the panel appears at once, exactly over the area (move the pointer around its edge: nothing shifts), with a hairline outline and the toolbar below, right-aligned. Draw a pen stroke, an arrow, a rectangle, text, a step and a blur; undo and redo with the buttons and ⌘Z/⇧⌘Z. The app you were in stays active (its menu bar stays). Return: "Copied"; paste into Preview (File → New from Clipboard); ⌥V lists it first, as "Screenshot". Again with a selection near the bottom of the screen (toolbar above), over the whole screen (toolbar inside, above the Dock), and on a second, 1× display. ⌘S saves a PNG (pick JPEG in the pop-up too); ⌘E opens the editor with everything drawn and Undo still working; Esc while a label is being typed ends the label, a second Esc closes. Upload, with an upload destination set.
19. **Desktop icons.** With icons and a widget on the desktop, turn on Settings → Capture → Hide desktop icons and widgets, then Capture Screen and Capture Area over the desktop: the frozen screen and the shots show the wallpaper without them. Finder's icons are still on the desktop afterwards, and `defaults read com.apple.finder CreateDesktop` answers as before. Record Area and Translate Area still show them.
20. **Pins.** Pin a capture. Scroll over it (zoom), ⌥-scroll (opacity, the value shows in the middle), press 5 (50 %) and 0, ⌘+ and ⌘0, the arrows, ⌘C (⌥V shows the image), ⌘S. Right-click: drag the opacity slider; toggle Border and Shadow, and Lock Position (dragging no longer moves it). Turn on Click-Through: clicks reach the window underneath; hold ⌥⌘ over the pin, which outlines itself and can be dragged; let go and it lets clicks through again. Menu → Pinned shows the pin with "Click-through" under its name, its submenu toggles it, and Stop Click-Through on All Pins brings every pin back. Repeat ⌥⌘ with Accessibility revoked: the pin opens once the pointer moves.
21. **History search.** Open Capture History and type a word that appears in a capture: the capture shows the sentence with the word in bold. Search for a word near the bottom of a long scrolling capture.
22. **Step Capture.** Menu → Start Step Capture: the bar appears. Click a button in some app, then Capture Area around it: the bar counts 1 step and Quick Access does not open. Take a window capture and a screen capture. Close the bar with ×: the next capture shows "Step 4 captured". Menu → Finish Step Capture: the window lists four steps, the first with a red ring where you clicked. Give titles and notes, drag step 3 first, delete one with ⌫ and Undo it, Retake one, Annotate one, draw an arrow in the editor and drag the result onto the step (or ⌘C there and ⌘V on the step). Export each format: the PNG opens in Preview with numbered headers; the PDF has a page per step and bookmarks in the sidebar; the Markdown opens in an editor with its images; the HTML opens in Safari, also in dark mode. Close the window: no question after an export; after another edit it asks. Start again and Cancel with steps: it asks first.
23. **Editor: backgrounds and steps.** In the editor, open the background button: each tile shows the screenshot on it. Try Aurora, Linear with two colours and an angle, Picture → Desktop Picture (a system wallpaper works; one in ~/Desktop says to choose it instead, and no permission prompt appears), and Choose… an image with blur. Set an inset, the light and dark frames, the shadow sliders. Choose X (Twitter) and export a PNG: `sips -g pixelWidth -g pixelHeight` gives 1200 × 675; Instagram portrait gives 1080 × 1350; a custom 800 × 800 gives that. Move the position on a 9:16 canvas. Save the background with +, quit and reopen Oriel: it is still there; right-click deletes it. Then add four steps and delete the second: 1, 2, 3. Restart at 1 and add two: a second series. Square shape, A B C labels and a pointer: drag from a button to beside it, then drag its tip; the arrow keys move a selected step with its pointer.
24. **Smart tools and old documents.** On a screenshot with an email, a phone number, an IP address, a card number (4111 1111 1111 1111) and a made-up key (AKIAIOSFODNN7EXAMPLE): ⇧⌘R outlines each with its category; Links are off; Redact hides them all, fully, and one Undo brings them back. Simplify a Settings window: bars in the text's colours, the rest toned down. Smart Move on a Finder window: drag a block of text away and its place is filled with the background; delete one; Undo. Open an `.oriel` saved by build 26: it looks as it did; save it again and it opens.
25. **Video editor.** Turn on Settings → Recording → Highlight clicks (Remember clicks for Auto Zoom is on by default) and the camera bubble with Blur camera background. Record an area for 20 s, clicking in three places, two of them close together: the bubble shows you sharp in front of a blurred room (with a solid color, in front of the color), and the recordings folder has the movie and a `.oriel-clicks.json` file. In the finished panel, Copy Text on a frame with text: the text is copied (⌥V shows it) and a panel shows it. Press Edit…: the editor opens with the filmstrip. Space plays, J/K/L shuttle, ← → step. Auto Zoom: three zooms appear in the zoom lane, and the preview zooms in on each click just before it and pans between the two close ones, with no black edges. Drag a zoom, resize it, change its level and its focus area; ⌘Z undoes each step. Press I and O to trim; press M twice and rename the chapters; ↑ ↓ jump between them; Copy for YouTube pastes `00:00 …` lines. ⌘E: export an MP4 (HEVC, 1080p) with progress, cancel another to see nothing is left behind, then export a GIF. Open the MP4 in QuickTime Player (the chapter menu) and IINA: the chapters are there and the zooms play smoothly. Close the editor with unexported edits: it asks first. Drop another movie on the window: it opens. Menu → Open Video… opens any movie. During a recording, Settings → Recording → Video Effects… opens Control Center's Video Effects; with Portrait on, the bubble shows macOS's blur and Oriel's steps aside.
26. **Translate Under Cursor (⌃⇧U),** with Japanese as the target. A toast says it is on and a menu-bar icon appears. Rest the pointer on an English paragraph in Safari: after about half a second a tooltip below it shows "English → Japanese · …" and the translation of the whole paragraph. Move along the paragraph: it stays. Move to the next one and rest: it changes. Go back: the first translation is there at once (the provider's usage counter in Settings does not move). Scroll: it goes, and the new text under the pointer is translated. Type in a text field: it goes and does not come back until the pointer moves. Esc turns the mode off; ⌃⇧U turns it on again; the menu-bar icon's Stop turns it off. With Settings → Translation → Translate Under Cursor → "The line under the pointer", only the line is translated. In a full-screen app and on a second display the tooltip appears where the pointer is. Idle with the mode on, Activity Monitor shows Oriel at 0 % CPU. Without Screen Recording (a VM, or Oriel switched off in System Settings), ⌃⇧U shows the alert, its button opens the Screen & System Audio Recording pane, and the mode stays off.
27. **Translate File… and glossaries.** A text PDF of several pages translates page by page with progress; Stop halfway, then Resume finishes it; Export → Markdown has "## Page" headings. A scanned PDF says "read with OCR". Try a .docx with a bulleted list, a Markdown README with code, a web page saved from Safari (no network access while it is read) and an .srt: Export → SRT and → WebVTT play in QuickTime or IINA with the original timing. An image of a sign shows its translation drawn over it; Copy Image pastes it into Preview. Drop a .zip: the window explains. Drop a PDF on the side panel of an area translation: it opens in the file window. Then in Settings → Translation → Edit Glossaries…, add "Docs" with English → Japanese and "capture" → "キャプチャ"; import a CSV exported from Numbers (with é and ü) and one with `source_language` columns. Choose "Docs": an LLM provider uses the term; with Apple Translation the term appears as キャプチャ; with DeepL, the DeepL account lists the glossary, and editing a term replaces it at the next translation. Delete the project: its DeepL glossary goes. Quit and reopen Oriel: the glossaries are still there.
28. **Upload.** Settings → Upload → Add Destination → **S3-Compatible Storage** with a test bucket; Test Connection says an upload and a delete worked. Capture Area → Upload in Quick Access: a toast says the link was copied, and the link opens the image. In Settings, the earlier build's bucket (if you had one) is listed first, named after its host, as the default. Then:
    - **WebDAV** (a Nextcloud with an app password): Test Connection reports the folder, or that it will be made; upload twice, and both links work, each in its own random folder. With a wrong password, the error says the server refused it.
    - **SFTP** to a server you reach with a key: Test Connection names the remote folder; a full-screen capture uploads with an indeterminate bar, and the link (with a public URL set) opens the image. A host you never connected to: the error says to run `ssh host` once. A key file that does not exist: the error says so.
    - **Shared folder** on a mounted SMB share: the link is `smb://server/share/…`. Rename the folder in Finder and upload again: it still lands there, and Settings shows the new name. Eject the share: the error says to connect in Finder.
    - **Dropbox:** create an App folder app with the three permissions, paste the key, Sign In…: Dropbox asks for access, and Settings shows your name and email. Upload with "The file itself": the link shows the image in the browser. Untick `sharing.write`, sign out and in: Settings says which permission is missing.
    - **Google Drive:** an iOS client, Sign In… with the Drive box ticked: Settings shows your account. Upload: an "Oriel" folder appears in My Drive with the file, shared with anyone with the link. Sign in again leaving the Drive box clear: Settings says Drive access wasn't allowed. Paste a folder of your own: Settings warns to choose All of Google Drive; do, sign in again, upload.
    - **Progress and Cancel:** throttle the network (Network Link Conditioner, 3G) and upload to Dropbox: the panel shows "x MB of y MB"; ⓧ cancels and the toast says so. Two uploads at once stack their panels. Turn off **Copy the link after uploading**: the toast says only where it was uploaded. Set **Copy the link as** Markdown: the clipboard has `![name](link)`. VoiceOver reads the panel's title and its Cancel button.
29. **Disk Space Analyzer.** Choose Disk Space Analyzer in the menu: the window lists Macintosh HD with used, purgeable and free space, any external drive, iCloud Drive and other cloud folders. Select Macintosh HD: the overview explains purgeable space and lists local snapshots. Scan it: Oriel first says macOS may ask about some folders; allow Desktop, Documents and Downloads when asked (each once, with Oriel's text). The window stays responsive while it scans, and the total matches Finder's used space (Get Info on Macintosh HD), with the macOS folder and an "Other space" block. Without Full Disk Access a note says how many folders macOS protects, and **Full Disk Access…** opens that pane; grant it, quit and reopen Oriel, scan again: that note shrinks to what System Integrity Protection guards (item 42 covers the owner-only folders).
30. **Disk views.** In the sunburst, hover (a tip with name, size and share), click into Users → your home, click the center to go back; the change fades. In the treemap, double-click into a folder and press ⌘↑; then use the breadcrumbs. ⌘3 List: sort by size and name; ⌘4 Biggest Files: filter by Videos and 1 GB or more, and right-click the header to show Last Used; ⌘5 Old Files lists files not used for a year; ⌘6 Duplicates on a folder with a copied file (Finder → Duplicate makes a clone, so "frees" stays small; copy it to another volume and back for a real duplicate), Mark All but Newest; ⌘7 File Types: a row opens Biggest Files of that kind; ⌘8 Measure Space Hogs. Search for a file name. VoiceOver reads the chart's segments and the rows. In Dark Mode every view stays readable.
31. **Disk: removing and moving.** In a test folder in Downloads: select two files, ⌘⌫: the confirmation names two items and their size; they go to the Trash and the scan updates; ⌘Z brings them back. ⌥⌘⌫ on a file asks twice and deletes it. Try ⌘⌫ on Downloads itself, on ~/Library/Caches, on Safari.app and on a file inside an app: each is refused with a reason. Move a 2 GB folder to an external drive: progress with Stop; the copy is complete and the original gone. Copy a large file to another folder on the same disk: it is instant (a clone). Change something in the scanned folder in Finder: the scan follows it a few seconds later.
32. **Disk: reclaiming, history and CLI.** Scan iCloud Drive: downloaded and cloud-only space are shown; **Remove Download** on a downloaded file makes it cloud-only (Finder shows the cloud icon) and the space comes back. On Macintosh HD with local snapshots, **Remove Snapshots…** asks for an administrator's password and the snapshots go (`tmutil listlocalsnapshots /`). **Open Trash** and **Storage Settings…** open Finder and System Settings; eject an external drive from its overview. Scan a folder, add a 1 GB file to it, scan again: a note says it grew by 1 GB and **Show Changes…** names the folder. Export as CSV and open it in Numbers. In Terminal: `oriel disk volumes`, `oriel disk scan ~/Downloads --top 5` and `oriel disk scan ~/Downloads --json`. Close the window: within a few seconds Oriel is back at its idle memory, at 0 % CPU.
33. **Activity Monitor** (about 10 minutes). Menu → Activity Monitor: the window opens on CPU with "Measuring…" for a moment, then the list; within two seconds % CPU fills in, other users' processes included (WindowServer, mds_stores); `open oriel://activity` brings it forward.
    - ⌘2 to ⌘5: each tab has its columns and summary; Network shows per-process rates after a second; Energy lists apps keeping the Mac awake; GPU % shows under CPU.
    - In Terminal run `yes > /dev/null`: within 10 seconds `yes` has a flame, an orange % CPU and a chip in the strip; click the chip and it is selected. ⌥⌘Q → Quit ends it; run it again and choose Force Quit.
    - View → All Processes, Hierarchically: kernel_task → launchd → …; Terminal → login → zsh. View → Apps with Their Helpers: Safari or Chrome with its helpers, totals on the app's row.
    - Search "chrome": only Chrome's processes; Esc clears. Sort by Memory, hide Real Memory from the header's menu, quit and reopen Oriel: the window, the column and the order are as left.
    - Quit on WindowServer, loginwindow and Oriel itself: each is refused with the reason. Quit on `mds_stores` (root): the administrator confirmation, then macOS's password prompt; Cancel it and nothing happens.
    - On one of your processes, More → Sample Process…: the report opens in TextEdit after a few seconds. ⌘I shows the live graphs, the parent link, the code signature (Developer ID for Chrome, Apple for Finder), and arguments and open files for your own processes.
    - Minimize the window for a minute and bring it back: the graphs continue. Close it: `pgrep -l 'ps|nettop'` shows nothing from Oriel, and Oriel's CPU drops to 0. Settings → Activity Monitor → Show only my processes: other users' rows and the system views go.
    - `oriel top`, `oriel top --sort memory --limit 5` and `oriel top --sort network --json` answer within a few seconds; `oriel top --sort speed` exits 64. With VoiceOver, the rows, the strip's chips and the graphs have labels.
34. **Color Picker.** Press ⌃⇧C over Safari: the loupe appears; click an orange: the panel opens, the HEX is on the clipboard (paste it into TextEdit) and its row says Copied. Press 8: the SwiftUI value is copied. Pick a vivid color from a Display P3 image: *Outside sRGB* appears and the code rows say "· P3". Click in another app: the panel stays and typing still goes to that app; click a format row: it copies without taking the keyboard. Type `rgb(0 128 255)` in the HEX field and press Return. Add the color to a new palette, rename it, drag its swatches around and into a second palette, delete a palette and Undo. Export a palette as CSS, JSON and `.clr`, and open the `.clr` in the system Colors panel (TextEdit → Format → Show Colors → Color Palettes → Open). On Contrast, pick the text color from the screen, swap, and check that #767676 on #FFFFFF reads 4.54:1 with AA Pass and AAA Fail. Quit and reopen Oriel: Recent, the palettes and the contrast colors are still there, and the panel opens where it was.
35. **Screen Ruler.** With Screen Recording, press ⌃⇧M on a two-display setup (Retina and 1×). Dimensions: hover a Finder toolbar button and the lines stop at its edges; click to keep the box; draw a box around an icon and it fits it; ⌘C and paste ("… × … pt"); U shows pixels; `]` and `[` change the tolerance in the bar. Tab to Measure Area: drag across a window (edges snap; ⌘ stops it), ⇧ for a square, the arrows nudge, Return closes and the toast shows what was copied. Tab to Guides: add vertical and horizontal guides, drag one, remove one with a right-click. Do the same on the second display. Switch to another app with ⌘⇥: the ruler closes. Without Screen Recording ("Oriel Debug" before granting it): Measure Area and Guides work, Dimensions shows its card, and Return opens System Settings.
36. **Window Tiling** (about 15 minutes, two displays if you have them).
    - In Safari press ⌃⌥←: the window fills the left half; again: two thirds, then one third, then a half. ⌃⌥→, ⌃⌥↑ and ⌃⌥↓ do the same for the other halves. Quarters (⌃⌥U, I, J, K), thirds (⌃⌥D, F, G) and two thirds (⌃⌥E, T): two windows side by side meet exactly. ⌃⌥↩, ⌃⌥⇧↑ and ⌃⌥C; Almost Maximize from the menu bar's Window Tiling menu, which shows every layout with its picture and shortcut and arranges the app in front, not Oriel.
    - Drag a window somewhere, press ⌃⌥← then ⌃⌥↩, then ⌃⌥⌫: it is back where you dragged it; ⌃⌥⌫ again beeps. ⌃⌥= and ⌃⌥- grow and shrink the window; on the left half it grows to the right only.
    - With two displays, ⌃⌥⌘→ on a left-half window: the left half of the other display; on an untiled window, the same size and place; ⌃⌥⌘← brings it back. With one display the two items are dimmed in the menu.
    - Terminal's bottom half sits on the bottom edge; System Settings (fixed width) on the right half stays against the right edge. Chrome and VS Code, with VoiceOver on and off: halves land at once, at the right size. A full-screen window: "Full-screen windows can't be tiled". With a Save sheet open in TextEdit, ⌃⌥←: the window moves with its sheet, or a toast asks to close the sheet first; note which.
    - Settings → Window Tiling → Gap 12 pt: halves and quarters are 12 points apart and from the edges. Remove Oriel from Accessibility and press ⌃⌥←: the notice appears for a few seconds without taking the focus, and Open System Settings opens the right pane. Turn Window Tiling off in General → Tools: ⌃⌥← reaches the app in front again, and the menu has no Window Tiling submenu.
37. **Shortcuts app and Spotlight.** In the Shortcuts app, find Oriel's actions: run Take a Screenshot (Screen) into Quick Look; Translate Text in an Image on a screenshot of Japanese text; Focus Window on a window on another Space; Run Oriel Command with Window Tiling's Left Half. In Spotlight, type "Take a screenshot with Oriel".
38. **Settings sync** (two Macs). Turn sync on, first on Mac A, then on Mac B: B adopts A's shortcuts. Change the Clipboard History item limit on B: within a minute A shows it. Turn a tool off on A: B turns it off too. The capture save folder stays different on each Mac.
39. **Opening and staying open.** Quit Oriel and open it from Applications: Settings opens. Close it with its close button: Oriel stays in the menu bar, ⌥⇥ opens the switcher and switches. Do the same with Disk Space Analyzer and Activity Monitor open, then closed: Oriel keeps running (`pgrep -lf Oriel`), and Console → Crash Reports has no new `Oriel-*.ips`. Open Oriel from Applications while it runs: Settings comes forward. With **Open Oriel at login** on, log out and back in: Oriel is in the menu bar and no window opens.
40. **The switcher and Oriel's windows.** With Settings and Disk Space Analyzer open, ⌥⇥ lists both with titles and thumbnails, and choosing one brings it forward. In the switcher, ⌘W closes one, ⌘M minimizes the other, and choosing the minimized one brings it back; ⌘Q on an Oriel window does nothing. With a keep-open shortcut, leave the switcher open while an Oriel window opens and closes: the list follows, nothing crashes.
41. **Window Tiling on Oriel's windows.** With Settings in front, ⌃⌥← fills the left half, ⌃⌥↩ maximizes and ⌃⌥⌫ restores it; the same on the Disk Space Analyzer window.
42. **Disk: closed folders and Read as Administrator.** With Full Disk Access granted and Oriel reopened, scan Macintosh HD. The folder **macOS** holds System, usr, bin, sbin and a Preboot block, and comes to about what `df -h` shows for `/`, Preboot, VM and Update together; there is no "macOS (system)" block. The notes say how many folders only macOS or another user can open and how many macOS protects, and only the second offers Full Disk Access. Choose **Read as Administrator…**: with the helper not installed, Oriel offers Install Helper…, Just This Scan and Cancel. Choose **Just This Scan**: macOS's password prompt names Oriel and the folders' count; Cancel changes nothing. Enter the password: within seconds the first note goes (or keeps only what System Integrity Protection guards); `.Spotlight-V100`, `.fseventsd` and `/private/var/db` have sizes, and the inspector says they were read with an administrator's rights; Move to Trash on one of them is refused with the reason; Other space shrank by about what was added.
43. **Disk: macOS in the lists.** Biggest Files of the whole scan lists macOS's largest files with the rest (the swap files and Preboot's large files, if any); Old Files leaves them out until you open the macOS folder; Duplicates never shows them. Move to Trash on a file in `/System` or on `/.file` is refused. Settings → Disk Space Analyzer → turn off **Count macOS's own files one by one** and scan again: one "macOS (system)" block of about the folder's size, and the scan takes a few seconds less.
44. **Disk: Read as Administrator's helper.** Scan Macintosh HD, choose **Read as Administrator…** → **Install Helper…**: System Settings opens on Login Items & Extensions, and a notification says Oriel added a background item. Switch on Oriel under Allow in the Background: macOS asks for an administrator's password. Come back to Oriel: within a moment the folders are read, without another prompt. Scan again: the owner-only note shows "Reading … as an administrator…" for a moment and goes, with no prompt. `oriel disk scan /System/Volumes/Data/private/var/db --json` reports `administratorOnlyFolders` close to 0. Settings → Disk Space Analyzer → Read as Administrator says the helper is allowed; turn off **Read them in every scan** and scan again: the note stays until you choose Read as Administrator… (no prompt). **Remove Helper**: Login Items no longer lists it, and Read as Administrator… offers the helper again.
